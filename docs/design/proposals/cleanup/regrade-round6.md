## Comparison with all five peer languages

Comparison is of idiomatic solutions, not spelling translations, and uses the same environmental assumptions. General baseline: Python's managed region and `finally` make restoration expressible; Rust has lexical drop scopes but fallible completion normally needs an explicit operation; Go's defer is function-scoped and does not check a discarded close result; Swift has scope-exit defer; TypeScript has `using`/`await using` and suppression of coincident failures. These facts support judgments below, not a numerical claim of usability superiority. Primary references: [Python](https://docs.python.org/3/reference/compound_stmts.html#the-with-statement), [Rust](https://doc.rust-lang.org/stable/reference/destructors.html#drop-scopes), [Go](https://go.dev/ref/spec#Defer_statements), [Swift](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/swiftlang/swift-book/main/TSPL.docc/ReferenceManual/Statements.md), [TypeScript](https://www.typescriptlang.org/docs/handbook/release-notes/typescript-5-2.html).

Each cell identifies the relevant comparator and the practical difference; it does not imply a bundled identical API.

Dewy's particular advantage is automatic, typed fallible cleanup rather than merely automatic destruction. Python and TypeScript already provide straightforward local exception recovery around managed regions; Dewy's pending Q3 and helper requirement are a real completeness cost. The functional rendering and ordinary-result timing rewrites compare well without defer. The FFI case supports a local convenience claim for defer, not a safety claim over owners.
