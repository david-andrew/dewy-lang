# Dogfood round 7

**Floor: 3.** Fixed on paper: syntactic attachment, value-versus-cleanup ordering, the seven review traces, the fallible-inline-guard hole, integer obligations, and conservative marker checking. Material costs remain: scope/arm grouping, selective error-as-data behavior, closure/parallel ownership discoverability, foreign contracts, manual channel completion, and verification of borrow/effect preservation through the concrete local callback owner before removing defer.

This is the author's regrade, informed by the independent round-6 review; it is not a new independent review or reader test. Programs are paper specifications, not executed compiler tests. O/D/C/S/K means obvious/discoverable/concise/safe by default/complete, each 1–5. A 5 is complete for the stated scenario and contracts, not proof of production implementation. Intentional bad before-versions are diagnostic cases; grade the recommended version, with their maintenance risks included where relevant. Comparison 27 includes a concrete local shared-callback owner, a rejected escape and a wrong-copy control; its compiler lifetime/effect checks are a gate, not a claimed executed test.

The original five-language comparison remains a baseline. A full compact comparison for this revision follows the scores. Every score below 5 has its own label and source-line evidence. The two all-5 rows cite all five dimensions. All numbered references are to programs-r7, with one-based lines.

## Changes from round 6, without grade inflation

- 01's integer obligation is fixed by exact int. No invented bounded int64 proof.
- 08/19 and new 20–24 now settle parse, selection order, selective coverage and the inline hole; teaching costs stay at 3 even where completeness reaches 5.
- 09's scoped-parallel gap is addressed by 23. That new program still gets K4 for the owning-output case it does not instantiate.
- 11 adds two crossed owners, not only one labeled jump.
- 12 and 18 use the supplied errors design, rather than keep their old unresolved-pair language. Remainder forwarding is now real source, not a promised future handler.
- 14 receives the independent review's all-5 grade **for ordinary results only**; no panic/exit requirement was quietly added or removed from the code.
- 16 does not get higher safety because it now has an owner. The same unsafe/trusted-contract boundary remains, and its owner matches defer's capability.
- 25 changes the timing evidence against the recommendation: totals are 15/15/17, not 15/15/14.
- 27 now supplies the concrete shared-callback owner rather than stopping at the wrong-copy objection. This supports dropping defer in the tested scenarios, conditional on proving the existing nonescape/effect contracts through the local aggregate before implementation removal; it is not a claim of unrestricted borrowed-owner storage.

## What would improve the floor

- Cold-read the grouped body/arm and selected-value versus cleanup cases; no fresh readers were launched here and no 5 for intuitive discoverability is invented.
- Verify a real foreign adapter's full effect/serialization contract; both owner and defer currently share that missing evidence.
- Validate the concrete local callback owner in 15/27, including rejected escape and preserved effects, before deleting defer support. A copied value, erased borrow or unsafe pointer is not an equal substitute.
- Demonstrate owning result/error payloads and explicit-allocation-exhaustion grouping in compiler/runtime tests once implementation is authorized; the supplied errors design already names that K3 limitation.

The remaining 3s are substantive tradeoffs or gaps. This is an honestly dogfooded paper packet with review fixes, not an all-5 entry ready to copy into open_decisions.md.
