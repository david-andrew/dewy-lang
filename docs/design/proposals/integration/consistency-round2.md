Checked distinctions: `strict_` exposes failures; `secure_` promises unpredictability and secret results; `fast_` identifies the nonsecure byte API. They need not be one prefix. `ShapeError`, `PathEscape`, `NoEntropy` and `Unsent<T>` describe different failures; preserve their payloads rather than imposing one error suffix. `$no_/$prove_/$allow_/$explicit_` are policy families; `$program.*` is singleton configuration, `$parallel.*` lexical tuning, and `$discardable_result` a declaration contract.

`.start/.end` are written bounds; `IntRange.first/last` are actual members. P’s lowercase `iterable<T>` does not make noninteger ranges iterable. `Task` and `Playing` satisfy the handle-return rule; their synchronization-only `.wait` returning `void | Canceled` is not a violation. P expressly forbids task value results and routes failures through the group. No new compiler-known type-name convention, second literal-validation mechanism, or live `{x:spec}` example was found; occurrences of the latter describe rejected/current-compiler behavior. The counted-hole mechanism remains unfinished rather than a justified alternative validator.
